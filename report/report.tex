\section{Introduction}
% charging law and time constant go here

This document introduces the charging behavior of a resistor-capacitor (RC) network. When a capacitor C is charged through a series resistor R from a constant supply $V_0$, the capacitor voltage rises according to

\begin{equation}
    V(t) = V_0(1-e^{-t/RC})\text{,}
    \label{chaging_voltage}
\end{equation}

\noindent
where the product RC has units of seconds and is called the time constant, $\tau=RC$

\section{Voltage at the Time Constant}

Substituting $t = \tau$ into \ref{chaging_voltage}:

\begin{equation}
    \begin{align}
        V(\tau) &= V_0\left(1-e^{-\tau/RC}\right)\\
                 &= V_0\left(1-e^{-RC/RC}\right) \qquad \text{since } \tau=RC\\
                &= V_0\left(1-e^{-1}\right)\\
                &\approx V_0\left(1-0.368\right)\\
                &\approx 0.632V_0
   \end{align}
\end{equation}

\noindent
The exponent canceling to -1 in \ref{chaging_voltage_at_t_const} is why the 0.632 is universal -- R and C dropped out of the calculation before the final line, so this fraction holds for any RC network. Substituting $V_0=5.0V$:

\begin{equation}
    V(\tau) \approx 0.632 \times 5.0V \approx 3.16V\text{,}
\end{equation}
